The Oklahoma City market

Oklahoma City sits at the center of one of the most tornado-active regions in the country, and that changes the shape of roofing demand here compared to the hail-driven markets we work in elsewhere. A hailstorm damages a roof. A tornado can remove one entirely, along with the rest of the structure around it, and that is a fundamentally different customer than a homeowner comparing hail-repair quotes.

An emergency job, not a comparison-shopping job

A homeowner with tornado damage needs a tarp on the roof the same day, before the next rain gets inside an already-open structure. That immediate response, not the eventual rebuild quote, is often what decides which roofer gets the larger job weeks later once the insurance and public-adjuster process plays out. A marketing plan built only around scheduling routine hail inspections has no answer for this moment, and loses the relationship before the real job ever gets discussed.

Rebuilds, not just reroofs

A catastrophic wind or tornado loss frequently means a full structural rebuild alongside the roof, involving a public adjuster, a larger insurance claim, and a longer timeline than a standard reroofing job. Sales messaging built around a quick, simple hail-repair offer undersells what a roofer capable of managing this larger process can actually charge for, and undersells the trust a homeowner needs from a contractor handling their entire claim, not just their shingles.

Insurance costs are already part of the conversation

Oklahoma has ranked among the states with the fastest-rising homeowners insurance premiums nationally, driven directly by repeated severe convective storm and tornado losses. Homeowners here are already primed to think about the cost of filing a claim versus the cost of paying out of pocket, and honest messaging about that tradeoff builds more trust than pretending the insurance conversation is not happening.

What this means for the marketing system

Build a fast-dispatch emergency-tarping offer that can go live the moment a tornado warning passes, separate from the standard storm-season ad campaign that handles routine hail inspections. The roofer who is reachable and mobilized within hours captures the relationship first, and the bigger rebuild conversation follows from that first response, not the other way around.

Questions

Frequently asked

How is marketing for tornado damage different from marketing for hail damage?

A hail claim is usually a comparison-shopping decision made over days or weeks. A tornado total loss is an emergency: the homeowner needs a tarp on the roof today, then a much larger rebuild conversation over the following months, often alongside a public adjuster. Marketing that only targets the routine hail-inspection funnel misses this entire emergency-response category.

Why is home insurance so expensive in Oklahoma City right now?

Oklahoma has ranked among the states with the fastest-rising homeowners insurance premiums in the country, driven directly by repeated severe convective storm and tornado losses. That cost pressure changes how homeowners think about filing a claim versus paying out of pocket, and marketing needs to speak to that calculation honestly.

Do OKC roofers need an emergency-response offer, not just an inspection offer?

Yes, if they want to capture the highest-value jobs. A company that can be reached and dispatched for same-day tarping after a tornado warning has passed captures the relationship early, before a homeowner starts calling around for a full rebuild quote. That first same-day response is often what decides who gets the larger job later.
