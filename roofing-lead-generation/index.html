Overview

Shared leads from Angi or Networx arrive in four inboxes at once and you compete on speed and price. That is not a pipeline, it is an auction. This service is about building the asset that makes the auction unnecessary.

The economics nobody shows you

A roofing lead is not a lead until someone answers the phone. The metric that matters is not cost per lead, it is cost per booked job. The gap between those two numbers is where roofing companies quietly lose most of their marketing budget. We measure the second one, which sometimes means telling you that your marketing is fine and your intake is broken. We broke down the real math in what a roofing lead actually costs.

Because the fastest response wins the job more often than the best price does.

Most homeowners are not ready today. The ones you follow up with are the ones you close in ninety days.

Speed to lead

Roofing leads decay fast. A homeowner with a leaking roof calls the next company on the list within minutes. If your response time is measured in hours, you are paying to generate leads for whoever answers faster. Fixing this is usually the single cheapest improvement available to a roofing company, and it is the first thing we look at.

Questions

Frequently asked

Should I buy roofing leads?

As a stopgap while you build your own pipeline, sometimes. As a long-term strategy, no. Shared leads are sold to your competitors simultaneously, the price rises every year, and you build no asset. Every dollar spent there is rented; every dollar spent on your own visibility is owned.

What is a roofing lead actually worth?

Take your average job value, multiply by your close rate, and that is what a lead is worth to you. Most roofers have never done that calculation, which is why they cannot tell whether their marketing is working. We do it on the first call, with your numbers.
